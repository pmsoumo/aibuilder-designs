What makes a great first-time user experience?

Summary
A great first-time experience lets the user do the thing they came to do without explaining. The core principle: "show, don't teach." Tutorial-itis is the enemy — the product should never stop to teach before it's earned the right to be taught.

What makes a great first-time user experience? I've been thinking about this all week, mostly in the context of dolfyn but the question is bigger than that.

A great first-time experience doesn't explain. It doesn't onboard. It just lets the user do the thing they came to do, and shows them a glimpse of why it was worth coming.

The risk is the opposite: tutorial-itis, where the product stops to teach before it's earned the right to be taught.

Takeaways
  • First impressions matter more than features — the user decides in 60 seconds whether to stay.
  • "Show, don't teach" is a filter for every design decision: does this let the user do the thing, or does it stop to explain?
  • Tutorial-itis shows up as a product trying to teach before the user has felt anything worth remembering.

You saw this in your own note from last week — the "write-first" decision was the same insight: don't explain the notebook, open it.
