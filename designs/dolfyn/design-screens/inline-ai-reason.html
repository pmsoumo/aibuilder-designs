Critique
Hidden assumptions

You assume the user knows what they want to do on arrival. But many first-time users don't have a goal — they're exploring. "Let them do the thing" presupposes they know what the thing is.

Socratic Questioning — Paul & Elder, 2007

Cognitive biases

Survivorship bias: you're reasoning from products that got it right (and you remember). What about the products that "just let you do the thing" but failed because the thing wasn't obvious?

Kahneman, Thinking Fast and Slow, Ch. 18

Blind spots

You haven't addressed the case where "the thing" requires setup. Some products need configuration before the user can do anything useful. How do you show without explaining in that scenario?

Christensen, Jobs to be Done, Ch. 2

Second-order effects

If you never explain, power users may never discover advanced features. The "show, don't teach" principle might optimize for first-time use at the cost of long-term depth.

Meadows, Thinking in Systems, Ch. 1
